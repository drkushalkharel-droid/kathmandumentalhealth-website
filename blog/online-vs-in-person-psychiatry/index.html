Online vs In-Person Psychiatry: What the Evidence Says and How to Choose

By Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicMedically reviewed by Dr. Kushal Kharel, Consultant Psychiatrist, 8 November 2026Published 8 November 20264 min readHow we write our guides

Many people now see a psychiatrist by video, especially Nepalis living abroad. This guide explains what research suggests about online care, who it suits, and when you should see a doctor in person.

Key takeaways

  • Reviews of telemental health find that video consultation for conditions such as depression and anxiety can work about as well as in-person care for many people.
  • Online care suits follow-ups, many first assessments, people abroad and people who cannot travel.
  • In-person or emergency care is better when someone is unsafe, severely unwell, or needs a physical examination.

  • What research suggests

What research suggests

Systematic reviews of telemental health, including video consultation, generally find that it is comparable to face-to-face care for diagnosis and for treatments such as cognitive behavioural therapy, with similar patient satisfaction. Results vary by condition and by study, and online care has been studied most for depression, anxiety and post-traumatic stress. It is not suitable for every situation.

Who online care suits

  • People living or working abroad who want a doctor who speaks Nepali.
  • People who live far from a clinic or find travel hard.
  • Follow-up visits after a first assessment.
  • People who feel more able to open up from a place where they feel safe.

When in-person care is better

  • Thoughts of ending your life, or a risk of harming yourself or others. Call emergency services or go to a hospital.
  • Severe symptoms such as confusion, hearing voices or being unable to care for yourself.
  • When a physical examination or tests are needed.
  • When you have no private place or reliable connection for a video call.

Making online care work

Choose a quiet, private place, use earphones, check your connection before the call and keep a list of your medicines. Tell the doctor where you are, in case you need local help. Prescription rules differ between countries, so ask your doctor how medicine would be arranged where you live; see our guide on medicine abroad after an online consultation.

Frequently asked questions

Is a video consultation as good as a clinic visit?

For many people and conditions, research suggests it can be comparable. Your doctor will tell you if you need an in-person assessment.

Is online psychiatry private?

Yes, if you join from a private place. Your information is shared only with your consent, except where a life is at risk or the law requires it.

Can I start online and visit later?

Yes. Many people have a first online consultation and visit in person when they are in Kathmandu.

What if I am in a crisis?

Call your local emergency number or, in Nepal, the suicide prevention helpline 1166, Police 100 or Ambulance 102.
