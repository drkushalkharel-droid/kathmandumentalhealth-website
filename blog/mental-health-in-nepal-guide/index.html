Mental health in Nepal

Mental Health in Nepal: Common Conditions, Stigma and Where to Get Help

By Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicPublished 25 September 20263 min read

Mental health affects families in every part of Nepal, from students facing exam pressure to workers abroad, parents, and older people. Yet many wait years before asking for help. This guide explains common conditions, why people hesitate, and where care is available.

Key takeaways

  • Mental health conditions are common, medical and treatable.
  • Stigma and worry about "what people will say" are the main reasons people delay help.
  • Help is available in Kathmandu, through hospitals, private clinics, counselling and online care.
  • In an emergency, call Nepal Police 100, ambulance 102 or the helpline 1166.

What mental health means

Mental health is how we think, feel and cope with daily life. Like physical health, it can be good, can be affected by stress and illness, and can be treated. Having a mental health condition is not a weakness or a character flaw.

Common conditions

  • Depression: persistent low mood, loss of interest, tiredness, hopelessness.
  • Anxiety and panic: constant worry, racing heart, fear of losing control.
  • Sleep problems and stress or burnout, common among workers and students.
  • OCD, ADHD and other conditions that often start in childhood or youth.
  • Alcohol, tobacco and drug problems.
  • Serious illnesses such as bipolar disorder and schizophrenia.
  • Memory problems and dementia in older adults.

Why many people wait

Common reasons are fear of what family and neighbours will say (log kya bhanchhan), the belief that only "mad" people need a psychiatrist, cost, not knowing where to go, and hoping the problem will pass. The result is that treatable conditions become harder to treat. The reality is that seeking help early is a sign of strength, and visits are confidential.

Traditional and modern care

Many families first turn to religious or traditional healers for comfort, and that can be a source of support. Mental health conditions such as depression, psychosis and addiction also have medical treatments that work, so it is wise to see a doctor as well and not to delay.

Where to get help

  • Psychiatrists at private clinics, such as Kathmandu Mental Health Clinic, for assessment, medicine and therapy. Find one near you.
  • Hospital psychiatry departments and emergency departments for serious illness or crises. See clinic or hospital?
  • Counselling and psychology services for talk therapy.
  • Online consultation for people outside Kathmandu or abroad. Learn more.
  • Helplines: 1166 is Nepal's suicide prevention helpline.

How to start the conversation with family

Choose a calm moment, say how you feel in simple words, explain that it is a health problem and not a choice, and ask them to come to an appointment with you. Families often become the strongest supporters once they understand.

Signs it is time to seek help

See a doctor when low mood, worry, sleep problems, unusual thoughts or substance use last more than a few weeks or affect your work, studies or relationships. Seek urgent help if you think of harming yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Is mental illness caused by weakness or bad karma?

No. Mental illness has biological, psychological and social causes, including genes, brain chemistry, stress and life events. It is not caused by weakness, and it is not a punishment.

Can mental health treatment be done in Nepali?

Yes. Our doctors see patients in Nepali or English, in person and online.

Is what I tell a psychiatrist kept private?

Yes. Consultations are confidential, and information is shared only with your permission, except in rare cases of serious risk or where the law requires it.

Call or message us. We reply in Nepali or English, and all three of our doctors also consult online.
