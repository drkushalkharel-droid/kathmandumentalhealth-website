Overthinking: What Helps and When to Get Support

Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicMedically reviewed by Dr. Kushal Kharel, Consultant Psychiatrist · Online therapyUpdated 27 September 2026

Replaying a conversation or imagining everything that could go wrong can make it hard to sleep, work, study, or enjoy time with other people. Repeated thinking is common during stress. When it keeps going without helping you solve anything, a few small changes can help you respond differently.

What do people mean by “overthinking”?

Overthinking is an everyday phrase, not a diagnosis on its own. It can mean going over the past (“Why did I say that?”), worrying about the future (“What if I fail?”), or trying to make a perfect decision. Stress, poor sleep, anxiety, low mood, and difficult circumstances can all make these thought loops more likely.

An article cannot tell you whether you have an anxiety disorder or another condition. A clinician looks at what the thoughts are like, how long they have been happening, and how much they affect daily life.

Four ways to get unstuck

  1. Name the loop. Try: “I’m replaying that conversation” or “I’m predicting a bad outcome.” Naming a thought can make it easier to notice it without treating it as a fact.
  2. Ask what is in your control. If there is a useful next step, write it down and choose a small action. If there is nothing to do now, remind yourself that the question can wait.
  3. Set a short worry time. Write the worry down and choose a brief time later to think it through. When it returns, note it and gently return to what you were doing.
  4. Care for the basics. A steadier sleep routine, a walk, regular meals, and less late-day caffeine may support wellbeing. These steps do not replace care when distress is persistent or severe.

When might professional support help?

Consider talking with a mental health professional if worry is difficult to control, continues for weeks, disrupts sleep or concentration, affects relationships, or leads you to avoid normal activities. A clinician can explore whether stress, anxiety, depression, obsessive thoughts, a physical health issue, or more than one factor may be involved.

There are effective ways to treat mental health conditions. Depending on the person and the assessment, support may include talk therapy, practical strategies, medication, or a combination. Do not start, stop, or change psychiatric medicine based only on an online article.

When you need help now

If you may harm yourself or someone else, or cannot stay safe, seek urgent in-person help now: call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department. In Nepal, call Police 100, Ambulance 102, or the suicide prevention helpline 1166.
