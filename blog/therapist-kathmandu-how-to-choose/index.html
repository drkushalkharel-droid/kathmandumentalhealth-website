Choosing mental health care

Therapist in Kathmandu: How to Choose

Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicMedically reviewed by Dr. Kushal Kharel, Consultant PsychiatristUpdated 28 September 2026

Searching for the “best therapist in Kathmandu” can bring up many titles and approaches. There is no single best therapist for everyone. A useful choice is someone appropriately trained for your concern, clear about how they work, and with whom you feel safe enough to speak openly.

Start by identifying the kind of support you want

Therapy can help people work through anxiety, low mood, grief, relationship strain, trauma, stress, or changes in daily functioning. A therapist may use one approach or combine methods based on training and the person’s needs. The US National Institute of Mental Health describes psychotherapy as a range of treatments that help people work with troubling emotions, thoughts and behaviours.

If you are unsure which professional to see, our guide explains the difference between a psychiatrist, psychologist, therapist and counsellor.

Questions to ask before choosing a therapist

  • What is your training? Ask about qualifications, supervised experience and the groups or concerns they work with.
  • What approach do you use? Ask what sessions are like, what goals you may work toward, and how progress is reviewed.
  • Have you worked with this concern? A provider should explain their experience honestly and refer you if another service is a better fit.
  • How do privacy and records work? Ask who can access information, what confidentiality means, and the limited circumstances when information may need to be shared for safety or legal reasons.
  • What are the practical details? Confirm fees, session length, location or video platform, language and cancellation arrangements.

How to tell whether the fit is working

You should be treated respectfully and have space to ask questions. You and the therapist should be able to discuss goals and whether the approach is helping. Feeling nervous at the first session is common; it is also reasonable to ask for clarification, discuss discomfort, or seek another provider if the relationship does not feel right.

Therapy is not a promise of a quick fix. It can take time to understand a problem and decide which support is useful. If progress stalls, discuss that openly rather than assuming you have failed.

When a psychiatrist may be the right first contact

Consider a psychiatric assessment if symptoms are severe, continue to disrupt daily life, involve major changes in sleep or energy, include thoughts of self-harm, or may need medical evaluation or medication. A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who can assess mental and physical health factors, discuss treatment options and provide or coordinate therapy. Some people benefit from both a psychiatrist and a therapist.

Kathmandu Mental Health Clinic’s doctors are psychiatrists who also provide CBT and supportive counselling. If a specialised therapy or a clinical psychologist would suit you better, ask about referral options. See doctor qualifications and available services.

Getting support in Kathmandu

Before booking, ask whether the provider offers in-person sessions in a location you can reach or a video appointment if appropriate. Discuss language preferences and the fee before you confirm. If you need urgent help because you cannot stay safe, contact emergency services or go to an emergency department instead of waiting for a routine therapy appointment.
