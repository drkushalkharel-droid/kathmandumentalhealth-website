ADHD

Adult ADHD in Nepal: Signs, Assessment and Treatment

By Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicPublished 25 September 20262 min read

ADHD is often thought of as a childhood problem, but many adults in Nepal live with it without knowing. If you have always struggled to focus, finish tasks or sit still, and it affects work, study or relationships, an assessment may help.

Key takeaways

  • ADHD continues into adulthood for many people, and adults can be assessed and diagnosed.
  • Symptoms must have started in childhood, and they must cause real problems in daily life.
  • There is no blood test or scan. Assessment is a detailed interview with questionnaires.
  • Treatment can combine practical strategies, treating related conditions and, for some people, medicine.

What ADHD looks like in adults

Adult ADHD is less about running around and more about an unsettled mind. It affects attention, organisation, impulse control and, for some people, restlessness.

Common signs in adults

  • Difficulty keeping attention on tasks, easily distracted, losing track mid-conversation.
  • Starting many things but finishing few; chronic lateness and poor time estimation.
  • Forgetfulness: missing appointments, losing keys, phone or documents.
  • Impulsive decisions, interrupting others, spending or risk-taking.
  • Inner restlessness, difficulty relaxing, always "on the go".
  • Strong reactions and low frustration tolerance.
  • Work, study or relationship problems that seem out of proportion to your ability.

Is it ADHD, anxiety, depression or lack of sleep?

Poor concentration also comes from anxiety, depression, sleep loss, thyroid problems and substance use, and ADHD often occurs alongside them. That is why a careful assessment matters: treating the wrong problem will not help. See our guides to anxiety symptoms and insomnia.

How ADHD is assessed

The psychiatrist takes a detailed history of current problems and of childhood (ADHD symptoms begin before about age 12), asks about school and work, health, sleep and substance use, and uses questionnaires. With your permission, a parent, partner or old school reports can add useful information. The aim is to confirm ADHD and to find any other conditions that need treatment.

Treatment options

  • Understanding ADHD and how it affects you often brings relief in itself.
  • Practical skills: planning tools, reminders, breaking tasks down, routines, and coaching or CBT-based approaches.
  • Treating related problems such as anxiety, depression or poor sleep.
  • Medicine, for some people. Whether medicine is suitable, which one and how it is monitored are decisions for your doctor after assessment.

Everyday strategies for work and study

Write tasks down, use alarms, keep a fixed place for keys and documents, work in short focused blocks, reduce distractions such as phone notifications, and ask for clear written instructions. Regular sleep and exercise noticeably help focus.

When to seek an assessment

Consider an assessment if these patterns have lasted for years and cause real difficulty. Our psychiatrists assess ADHD in adults and in children and teenagers. Call or message us to book.

Frequently asked questions

Can adults be diagnosed with ADHD later in life?

Yes. Many adults are diagnosed in their 20s, 30s or later. Symptoms must have been present since childhood, even if they were not recognised then.

Is ADHD just laziness or lack of discipline?

No. ADHD is a recognised neurodevelopmental condition that affects how the brain manages attention and impulses. People with ADHD often work very hard.

Can ADHD be managed without medicine?

Some people manage well with strategies, skills training and treating related problems, and others benefit from medicine as well. Your doctor will discuss what suits you.
