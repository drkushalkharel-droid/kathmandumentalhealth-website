Can I Get Psychiatric Medicine Abroad After an Online Consultation?

By Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicMedically reviewed by Dr. Kushal Kharel, Consultant Psychiatrist, 8 November 2026Published 8 November 20264 min readHow we write our guides

If you live outside Nepal and see a psychiatrist in Nepal by video, the next question is how to get medicine. The honest answer is that it depends on the country and the medicine. This guide explains the safe way to approach it.

Key takeaways

  • Rules for prescriptions, pharmacies and bringing medicine across borders differ by country, and some medicines are controlled.
  • A prescription from Nepal may not be accepted by pharmacies where you live. Often the safest route is a local doctor who assesses you and prescribes under local rules.
  • Never start, stop or change a psychiatric medicine on your own, and check customs rules before posting or carrying medicine.

In this article

  • Why it depends on where you live

Why it depends on where you live

Each country decides who can prescribe, which medicines need a local prescription and which are controlled. Medicines for sleep and anxiety are often tightly regulated, and some are illegal to import without permission in certain countries. A prescription written in one country is frequently not valid in another.

The usual safe route

  1. Have the online consultation to understand your diagnosis and treatment options.
  2. Ask your doctor for a clinical summary you can share with a doctor where you live.
  3. See a local doctor who can assess you and prescribe under local rules, when medicine is needed.
  4. Keep your Nepal-based psychiatrist and your local doctor informed of each other's advice, with your consent.

When it is clinically appropriate, Dr. Kushal Kharel can coordinate with a local doctor in your country and share a clinical summary with your consent.

Carrying medicine across borders

  • Check the customs and health authority rules of the country you are entering before you travel.
  • Carry medicine in its original packaging with a doctor's letter or prescription.
  • Do not post psychiatric medicine internationally unless the rules clearly allow it.

Never stop suddenly

Stopping some psychiatric medicines abruptly can cause withdrawal or a relapse. If you cannot get your medicine for any reason, contact a doctor straight away rather than stopping on your own.

Frequently asked questions

Can you send medicine to me abroad?

We do not advise posting psychiatric medicine across borders. Rules differ and some medicines are controlled. We will discuss the safest option for your country.

Will a pharmacy abroad accept a Nepal prescription?

Often not. That is why a local doctor's prescription is usually needed.

Can I get a summary for my local doctor?

Yes, with your consent we can share a clinical summary with a doctor where you live.

What if I run out of medicine?

Contact a doctor promptly. Do not stop suddenly without advice.
