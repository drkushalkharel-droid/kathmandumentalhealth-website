Mental health in Nepal

Mental Health Services in Nepal: Where to Start

Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicMedically reviewed by Dr. Kushal Kharel, Consultant PsychiatristUpdated 28 September 2026

Finding the right mental health service can feel confusing. The best place to start depends on what you are experiencing, how much it affects daily life, and whether you need immediate safety support, a medical assessment, or someone to talk with.

What mental health services can include

Mental health care is not one service or one type of professional. It can include assessment by a doctor, talk therapy, practical support, medication when appropriate, follow-up, and help for family members. The World Health Organization describes good mental health systems as a connected range of accessible services, including care in general health settings and community support.

People may seek help for persistent anxiety or low mood, panic, intrusive thoughts, sleep problems, trauma, relationship stress, substance use, or changes in thinking and behaviour. You do not need to know the diagnosis before asking for an appointment.

Psychiatrist, psychologist or counsellor?

  • A psychiatrist is a medical doctor with specialist training in mental health. They can assess mental and physical health factors, diagnose, discuss medication, and provide or coordinate therapy.
  • A clinical psychologist is trained in psychological assessment and therapy. Psychologists do not prescribe medication in Nepal as part of their psychology role.
  • A counsellor or therapist may offer structured talking support. Training and areas of practice vary, so ask about qualifications and experience.

Some people work with more than one professional. If you are unsure, an initial consultation can help identify a suitable next step. Read our guide to choosing between a psychiatrist, psychologist and therapist.

How to find mental health care in Nepal

  1. Start with the level of need. For ongoing symptoms, arrange an appointment with a qualified mental health professional or a doctor you trust. For immediate danger or inability to stay safe, contact emergency services or go to an emergency department.
  2. Check the provider’s role and training. Ask what they are qualified to do, whether they have experience with your concern, and how they handle follow-up. Medical doctors’ registration can be checked through the Nepal Medical Council.
  3. Ask practical questions before booking. Confirm the fee, location or video platform, appointment length, language, privacy arrangements, and how to contact the service if you need to reschedule.
  4. Choose a format that is workable. In-person care may suit people who need examination or local support. Video consultation can make follow-up easier for people living elsewhere in Nepal, when the clinician agrees it is appropriate.

Mental health services in Kathmandu and online

Kathmandu Mental Health Clinic provides psychiatric assessment, treatment planning, medication review when clinically appropriate, and therapy including CBT. Appointments are available in person at the clinic’s Kathmandu locations; video consultations are also available across Nepal. The team can explain which doctor or format may fit when you contact the clinic. See our doctors and qualifications or how online consultations work.

A clinic appointment is outpatient care. It is not a substitute for emergency assessment, inpatient admission, or local emergency services when someone is at immediate risk.

When to seek urgent help

If you or someone with you may harm themselves or another person, cannot stay safe, or is severely confused or unwell, seek urgent in-person help now. Contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department. Do not wait for an online appointment.
