Anxiety

Anxiety Treatment in Nepal: Therapy, Medicines and What Actually Helps

By Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicPublished 25 September 20263 min read

"Anxiety medicine" and "best anxiety doctor in Kathmandu" are searches from people who want relief and want to do it safely. The good news is that anxiety disorders respond well to treatment. This guide explains the main options, in plain language, without telling you what to take.

Key takeaways

  • Anxiety disorders are common and treatable, usually with therapy, medicine or both.
  • CBT is a first-line treatment. Antidepressant-type medicines are often used, and take weeks to work.
  • Sedative tablets can relieve anxiety quickly but can cause dependence, so doctors use them carefully.
  • Never start, stop or share anxiety medicine without a doctor's advice.

Anxiety comes in different forms

Generalised anxiety (constant worry about many things), panic disorder (sudden attacks of intense fear), social anxiety (fear of judgement), specific phobias, and health anxiety are all forms of anxiety disorder. They overlap, and they can occur with depression. Getting the right label helps choose the right treatment.

Treatment 1: talking therapy (CBT)

Cognitive behavioural therapy helps you spot worrying thoughts, test them against reality and gradually face situations you avoid. Sessions are practical, with things to try between visits. CBT works for many people without medicine and also reduces the chance of anxiety returning. Our doctors provide CBT, and Dr. Sunita Lama is formally certified in it.

Treatment 2: medicine

  • Antidepressant-type medicines (such as SSRIs and SNRIs) are commonly used for anxiety disorders, even when you are not depressed. They are not addictive, but they take about two to six weeks to show their full effect, and mild side effects can appear early. They should not be stopped suddenly.
  • Short-term sedatives (benzodiazepines) calm anxiety quickly but can cause dependence and tolerance, so they are prescribed carefully and usually only for a short time.
  • Other medicines, such as beta-blockers for the physical symptoms of performance anxiety, suit only some people and only after a doctor checks your health.

Do not buy sedatives or anxiety tablets without a prescription, do not share tablets with family or friends, and do not combine them with alcohol. The right medicine, dose and duration depend on you.

Treatment 3: habits that support recovery

Regular sleep, daily movement, less caffeine and tobacco, limiting alcohol, and talking to someone you trust all reduce anxiety. Relaxation and breathing practice help many people. These support treatment rather than replace it.

Choosing an anxiety doctor

Look for a registered psychiatrist who listens, explains the diagnosis and your options, discusses side effects openly, and reviews progress. Our checklist for choosing a psychiatrist covers what to ask.

How long does treatment take?

Some people feel better within a few weeks; for others treatment takes months, and it is reviewed regularly. Doctors often continue medicine for some months after you feel better to reduce relapse, then discuss reducing it gradually.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the best medicine for anxiety?

There is no single best medicine. The choice depends on the type of anxiety, other health conditions, other medicines, and your preferences. A psychiatrist will explain the options and side effects.

Are anxiety tablets addictive?

Antidepressant-type medicines used for anxiety are not addictive. Sedatives such as benzodiazepines can cause dependence, so they are used carefully and usually short term.

Can anxiety be cured?

Many people recover fully or learn to manage anxiety so it no longer controls their life. Symptoms can return during stressful periods, and the skills learnt in therapy help you deal with that.
