How We Track Your Progress: PHQ-9 and GAD-7 in Psychiatric Care

By Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicMedically reviewed by Dr. Kushal Kharel, Consultant Psychiatrist, 8 November 2026Published 8 November 20263 min readHow we write our guides

Feeling better is hard to judge when you are in the middle of it. Short questionnaires such as the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 give you and your doctor a shared way to see whether treatment is working.

Key takeaways

  • The PHQ-9 measures symptoms of depression and the GAD-7 measures symptoms of anxiety over the last two weeks.
  • Repeating them over time shows whether a plan is helping, so treatment can be adjusted.
  • They are screening and tracking tools, not a diagnosis. You can try them privately on our self-check page.

  • What the questionnaires measure
  • How scores guide care
  • The safety question

What the questionnaires measure

The PHQ-9 has nine questions about low mood, loss of interest, sleep, energy, appetite, concentration, self-worth, restlessness and thoughts of self-harm. The GAD-7 has seven questions about nervousness, uncontrollable worry, restlessness and fear. Each answer is scored from 0 to 3 and the total falls into a band from minimal to severe.

How scores guide care

  • A baseline score at the first visit shows how severe symptoms are.
  • Repeating the questionnaire at follow-ups shows whether the score is falling.
  • If the score is not improving, the doctor can review the diagnosis, the dose, the therapy or other factors.

Scores never replace a clinical conversation. They are one piece of information alongside how you feel and function.

The safety question

Question 9 of the PHQ-9 asks about thoughts of being better off dead or of hurting yourself. Any answer above zero deserves attention. If you are in Nepal, you can call the suicide prevention helpline 1166, Police 100 or Ambulance 102. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number.

Try it privately

Our free self-check page lets you complete the PHQ-9, GAD-7 and AUDIT-C. Answers are scored in your browser and are not sent to us. Bring your result to a consultation if you would like to discuss it.

Frequently asked questions

Is the PHQ-9 a diagnosis?

No. It is a screening and tracking tool. Only a doctor can diagnose a condition after a full assessment.

How often should I repeat it?

Often every few weeks during treatment. Your doctor will advise.

Are my answers stored?

On our self-check page, no. Answers are scored in your browser only.

Which score means I need treatment?

Moderate and higher scores are usually a reason to see a doctor, but how you feel and function matters more than the number.
