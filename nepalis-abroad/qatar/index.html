Nepalis in Qatar: life, work and study

Qatar is hot for much of the year, and many jobs run through long shifts or the hottest hours of the day, which can wear down sleep and mood. Most Nepalis live in or near Doha and the industrial areas, often in company or shared accommodation.

Days off can be short, so a video session that fits around a shift can be easier than travelling to a clinic. Qatar uses one time zone all year, with no clock changes.

A video call is hard in a shared room. Some people join from a quiet corner of a park in the cooler months, from a private room at a friend's place, or from a break-time spot with headphones. Tell us where you plan to join from and we can suggest the quietest option, and a backup phone number in case the signal drops.

Common pressures for Nepalis in Qatar

These are common experiences, not a diagnosis. If several feel familiar and have lasted for weeks, it is worth talking to someone.

  • Long shifts, heat and night work that leave little time or energy for anything else.
  • Shared accommodation, where privacy for a phone or video call can be hard to find.
  • Being away from family for months or years, and missing weddings, festivals and illnesses at home.
  • Money worries: sending money home, debt taken to travel for work, and contract or paperwork stress.
  • Sleep that does not settle after night shifts.

Time difference between Qatar and Nepal

Nepal is at UTC+5:45 all year, with no daylight saving.

Where you areTime differenceEvening example
DohaNepal is 2 h 45 min ahead8 pm there = 10:45 pm in Nepal

The clocks in this country do not change during the year, and Nepal has no daylight saving either, so the gap stays the same.

If you finish a night shift in the morning, tell us. Early morning in Doha is mid-morning in Kathmandu, and we can look for a time around your rest.

The working week in Qatar is usually Sunday to Thursday, with Friday and Saturday as the weekend, but many workers have only one day off a week, so tell us which day is yours. During Ramadan, working hours and sleep change in many workplaces, and we can plan around them.

Frequently asked questions

Can I have a video consultation with a Nepali psychiatrist from Qatar?

Yes. Dr. Kushal Kharel, founder of Kathmandu Mental Health Clinic and a Consultant Psychiatrist (MD Neuropsychiatry, NMC No. 27199), provides online psychiatry video consultation for Nepalis in Qatar through Google Meet or WhatsApp video call, or by phone if video is not possible, in Nepali or English, and Dr. Sunita Lama and Dr. Bhishma K.C. also provide video consultation. What is the time difference between Qatar and Nepal?

What should I do if I need urgent help in Qatar?

Call the local emergency number now: 999. Kathmandu Mental Health Clinic is not an emergency service.

Can you prescribe medicine if I live in Qatar?

A prescription from Nepal is generally not valid abroad, so the doctor explains what is possible where you live. Where medicine is needed, we can help you prepare a summary for a doctor in Qatar, and we work with therapy and practical strategies in the meantime. A family member in Nepal can arrange payment for you if that is easier; we share the fee when you book.

I work night shifts in Qatar. Can I still see a doctor?

Yes. Tell us your shift pattern when you book and we will look for a time that fits, including after a night shift. If privacy in shared accommodation is difficult, mention it and we will plan around it.

Can my employer or agent see that I am speaking to a doctor?

We do not tell your employer, your agent or anyone else, and we share nothing without your permission. Use your own phone and a private place, and tell us if you need to plan the time so that you are not overheard.
