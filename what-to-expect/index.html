First visit

What to Expect at Your First Psychiatry Appointment

By Kathmandu Mental Health ClinicMedically reviewed by Dr. Kushal Kharel, Consultant Psychiatrist, 24 October 2026Published 21 October 20263 min readHow we write our guides

Starting is the hardest part. Here is exactly what happens, so there are no surprises.

Key takeaways

  • No referral or diagnosis needed. Just call or WhatsApp. Fees start at Rs 1,500.
  • The first visit is for listening and understanding, not rushing to a prescription.
  • You agree the plan with the doctor. Medicine is optional unless needed for safety.
  • Everything is confidential, and online visits work from anywhere.

Booking

  1. Message or call. WhatsApp or call +977 9714556408. Tell us briefly what is worrying you. You do not need a diagnosis or a referral.
  2. Choose a time. In person in Kathmandu, or online by Google Meet or WhatsApp video. Nepalis abroad can pick a time that suits their time zone, and a family member in Nepal can help arrange it.
  3. Come or join. Join from somewhere quiet and private if online.

The first conversation

The doctor listens first. Expect questions about what you are feeling and for how long, sleep, appetite, energy, work or study, relationships, alcohol or other substances, past illness, medicines you take, and family history. Some questions are personal. You can pause, skip or come back to anything.

Depending on your concerns, the doctor may ask you to fill in a short questionnaire, or suggest blood tests or a physical check to rule out other causes such as thyroid or vitamin problems.

Agreeing a plan together

You will be told, in plain language, what the doctor thinks is happening and what your options are: counselling or therapy, medicine, lifestyle steps, or a combination. Medicine is not always needed. If it is suggested, you will hear why, how it works, common side effects, how long it takes and how long it might continue. The decision is yours.

How long it takes

The first visit is usually longer than follow-ups because the doctor needs to understand you properly. Ask us for the length of your slot when you book. Follow-up visits are for checking progress, adjusting treatment and continuing therapy.

Fees

Consultation with brief counsellingRs 1,500
CBT, CBT with ERP or systematic desensitisationRs 2,500
Full counselling sessionRs 2,000

We confirm the fee for your visit when you book, so there are no surprises. ERP (exposure and response prevention) is the CBT method used for OCD.

Privacy and family

What you say stays confidential. Information is shared with family or other doctors only with your consent, except when there is a serious risk to someone's life. Family members are welcome for part or all of the visit if you want them, and equally you can come alone. You can ask for a doctor of a particular gender or language when booking.

What to bring or prepare

  • A list of current medicines and past prescriptions or reports, if any.
  • A few notes: when it started, what makes it better or worse, how you sleep.
  • Your questions. Write them down so you do not forget.
  • For online visits: a charged phone or laptop, a stable connection and headphones.

After the visit

You will leave with a clear plan and a date for follow-up. If anything worries you before then, contact the clinic. For emergencies, do not wait for an appointment: call Police 100, Ambulance 102 or the suicide prevention helpline 1166.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book directly. Doctors, schools and counsellors can also refer you.

Will I definitely get medicine?

No. The plan depends on your assessment and your preferences. Many people start with talking therapy and lifestyle changes.

Can I bring a family member?

Yes, if you wish. You can also come alone and decide later whether to involve family.

Is an online first visit as good as in person?

For most concerns, yes. The doctor can assess, plan treatment and follow up by video. Some situations, such as severe illness or risk to safety, are better seen in person.
