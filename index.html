Nepalis abroad: your questions.

20 answers for Nepalis abroad

Can I consult a psychiatrist in Kathmandu online from another country?

Yes. All three of our doctors provide online consultation and therapy, so you can meet them by private video call from anywhere with a reliable internet connection, in Nepali or English. It is designed for Nepalis living, studying or working overseas.

Which countries do you see patients from?

Nepalis anywhere in the world. Many people who reach out live in Qatar, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Malaysia, Japan, South Korea, Australia, the United Kingdom, Europe, the USA and Canada, but you do not need to be in one of these countries.

How does an online session work?

You book by phone or WhatsApp, we agree a time in your time zone, and you receive a private video-call link. On the day, join from your phone, tablet or computer. Your first session is a full assessment, and we agree on a plan together at the end.

What do I need for a video consultation?

A phone, tablet or computer with a camera and microphone, a stable internet connection, and a quiet, private place. Headphones help you hear clearly and keep the conversation private. Have a phone number ready as a backup in case the video drops.

How do we find a time that works across time zones?

Nepal is at UTC+5:45. Use the time finder above to see what a Kathmandu time means where you live, then tell us which hours are best for you, including after night shifts or before work. We will offer the closest slot we have.

Can you prescribe medicine if I live abroad?

Medicine rules differ from country to country, and a prescription from Nepal is generally not valid abroad. In your session, the doctor will explain what is possible in your situation. Where medicine is needed, we can help you prepare a clear summary to share with a doctor in your country, and we work with therapy and practical strategies in the meantime.

I work long shifts in the Gulf or Malaysia. Can I still get help?

Yes. Tell us your shift pattern and we will look for a time that fits, even if it is late at night or early morning in Nepal. If privacy in shared accommodation is difficult, mention it when booking and we will plan the session around it.

Is online therapy as effective as meeting in person?

For many people with depression, anxiety and stress, video therapy works about as well as face-to-face sessions. Some situations, such as a severe crisis or a need for a physical examination, need in-person or local care. Your doctor will tell you if that applies to you.

How do I pay from abroad?

We share the fee and the available payment options when you book, before your session. If it is easier, a family member in Nepal can arrange payment for you; just ask us when you book.

Are online sessions private?

Yes. Online sessions are as confidential as in-clinic visits, and we share nothing with anyone without your permission. Choose a private place and use headphones so you can speak freely.

Can my family in Nepal or my partner join the session?

Yes, if you would like. Parents, a spouse or a partner can join by video from Nepal or another country, which can be especially helpful for couples living far apart or when a family member is worried about you.

My family in Nepal is worried about me. Can they book for me?

They can call us to ask questions and arrange an appointment. As an adult, you need to agree to take part in the session yourself, and what you share stays private unless you decide otherwise.

I feel very low and alone abroad. What should I do right now?

If you are in danger or thinking of ending your life, call your local emergency number now (for example 911 in the USA and Canada, 999 in the UK, Qatar and the UAE, 000 in Australia, 112 in Europe), or call a crisis line: 988 in the USA, 9-8-8 in Canada, Samaritans 116 123 in the UK, Lifeline 13 11 14 in Australia. Find other countries at findahelpline.com. Then reach out to us or someone you trust. You do not have to face this alone.

Is homesickness a mental health problem?

Homesickness is a normal response to living far from family. It is worth talking to someone if it lasts for weeks and comes with low mood, poor sleep, loss of interest, isolation or constant worry. Early support usually makes things easier.

I am a student abroad. Can you help with exam stress and loneliness?

Yes. Students often face study pressure, culture shock, money worries and loneliness far from home. We offer counselling and, where needed, psychiatric assessment, in Nepali or English, at times that fit around your classes.

I grew up abroad and speak limited Nepali. Can I still come?

Yes. Sessions can be held in English. We understand Nepali family expectations and culture, which many people find helpful when talking about family, identity and belonging.

I am visiting Nepal soon. Can I see a doctor in person?

Yes. You can visit us in person at People's Plaza or Kalanki, and then continue with follow-ups online after you return. It helps to book ahead so we can reserve a suitable time during your trip.

Can I get a summary of my assessment for my local doctor or employer?

Where it is clinically appropriate and you ask for it, your doctor can prepare a summary of your assessment and treatment. Whether another doctor, employer or university accepts it is decided by that organisation.

What if my internet connection drops during the session?

We will try to reconnect straight away. If the connection keeps failing, we can continue by phone call. That is why we ask you to share a backup phone number when you book.

Will I be able to speak in Nepali about my feelings?

Yes. Speaking in your own language makes it easier to describe how you really feel, and this matters a great deal in mental health care. You can use Nepali, English, or a mix of both.

[ 05 — Questions ]

Questions people often ask.

What is Kathmandu Mental Health Clinic and Psychiatry Counseling Center?

It is a psychiatry and counselling clinic in Kathmandu, Nepal. Psychiatrists and counsellors here care for adults, teenagers and children with depression, anxiety, OCD, sleep problems, ADHD, addiction, memory problems and more. You can visit us in person at People's Plaza (New Road) or Kalanki, or meet us online by video from anywhere in the world. The clinic was founded by consultant psychiatrist Dr. Kushal Kharel, and all three of our doctors offer online consultation and therapy.

How do I book an appointment?

Call 9714556408 or message us on WhatsApp. Tell us briefly what is troubling you, whether you prefer to come in person or online, and a time that suits you. We will confirm the location, doctor and time. You do not need to explain everything on the phone.

Do I need a referral to see a psychiatrist?

No. You can book directly with us. Doctors, schools, counsellors and family members are also welcome to suggest that you come, but a referral letter is not required.

What happens at my first appointment?

Your first visit is a conversation, not a test. The doctor listens to what you are going through, asks about your sleep, mood, stress, health and family, and then explains in plain words what may be happening. Together you agree on a plan: counselling, medicine, or both. Nothing is started without your understanding and agreement.

Should I see a psychiatrist or a counsellor?

A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who can diagnose mental health conditions, prescribe medicine and also offer therapy. A counsellor or psychologist provides talk therapy and cannot prescribe medicine. If you are unsure, start with a consultation: we will tell you honestly which one you need, and many people benefit from both working together.

Who can come to the clinic?

Children, teenagers, adults and older adults, as well as couples and families. You can come alone or bring someone you trust. Parents or guardians can book for a child and join the first visit.

How much does a consultation cost?

The fee depends on the doctor and the type of visit, such as a first assessment, counselling session or online consultation. We tell you the fee before you book, so there are no surprises. Call 9714556408 to ask.

Is the clinic an emergency service?

No. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call Nepal Police on 100, an ambulance on 102, or the national suicide prevention helpline 1166, or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. If you are in another country, call that country's emergency number.

Where is the clinic located in Kathmandu?

We have two locations in Kathmandu: Dev Plaza, People's Plaza, New Road (नयाँ सडक), and Kalanki Bhatbhateni. You can also meet us by video from a place where you feel safe. Call 9714556408 to confirm which option suits you and which doctor is available. You can also open People's Plaza in Google Maps.

What are your opening hours?

The clinic is open on Saturdays from 5:00 AM to 11:00 PM. Because doctors' schedules differ by location and day, please call 9714556408 to confirm the time before you come. We are not a 24-hour service.

How long are appointments?

An initial assessment takes longer than a routine follow-up because it involves a fuller history, and the exact time varies with the complexity of your concerns. Ask when you call to book if you need to plan your day around it.

Will my appointment feel rushed?

We aim for a full conversation about your main concern, history and goals, not a quick prescription-only visit. First assessments in particular take the time needed to understand the whole picture before you and the doctor agree on a plan.

Do you accept insurance, or do I pay directly?

Most patients in Nepal pay directly, because insurance cover for psychiatric consultations, therapy and medicine is limited and inconsistent. If you have insurance, check with your provider what it covers before you start, and ask us about payment when you book.

Is there a psychiatrist near me in Kathmandu?

Kathmandu Mental Health Clinic has two locations, People's Plaza (New Road) and Kalanki Bhatbhateni, and patients also come from across the Kathmandu Valley, including Lalitpur, Bhaktapur and Balkot. If travel is difficult, all three of our doctors consult online. Call 9714556408 to book, or read our guide to finding a psychiatrist near you.

Is Kathmandu Mental Health Clinic a mental hospital?

No. We are an outpatient psychiatry and counselling clinic, and we are not an emergency service. If someone needs hospital admission, our doctors will explain why and advise where to go. See mental hospital or clinic: choosing the right level of care.

Which areas do you serve?

We see patients in person at People's Plaza (New Road) and Kalanki in Kathmandu, and people travel to us from across the valley, including Lalitpur, Bhaktapur and Balkot. Online consultation is available across Nepal and worldwide, in Nepali or English.

Can I get mental health care in Nepali?

Yes. Our doctors see patients in Nepali or English, and depending on the doctor also in Hindi, Bangla or Tamang, so you can describe how you feel in your own language. You can also read our Nepali guide, मानसिक स्वास्थ्य: लक्षण, कारण र उपचार.

Do you offer home visits for psychiatry?

Yes, on request. Home visits suit people who cannot easily travel, such as older adults or people too unwell or anxious to leave home. Call 9714556408 to ask about availability, the area, the timing and the fee for your address. Read our guide to home visit psychiatry.

Where can I read patient reviews of the clinic?

What is neuropsychiatry, and do your doctors have this training?

Neuropsychiatry is the branch of psychiatry that focuses on mental health conditions linked to the structure and function of the brain, such as dementia, delirium, and psychiatric symptoms that arise from neurological conditions. It bridges psychiatry and neurology. All three of our doctors hold an MD in Neuropsychiatry.

What does a psychiatrist do in Nepal?

A psychiatrist in Nepal is a medical doctor who diagnoses and treats mental health conditions such as anxiety, depression, OCD, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, ADHD and addiction. At our clinic, psychiatrists provide assessment, diagnosis, medicine management and psychotherapy, in person in Kathmandu or online.

What mental health conditions do you treat?

We treat depression, anxiety, panic attacks, OCD, sleep problems and insomnia, stress and burnout, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia and other serious mental illness, ADHD and autism concerns in children, alcohol, tobacco and drug addiction, memory problems in older adults, and relationship or family difficulties. Will I have to take medicine?

Not always. Many people improve with counselling and practical changes alone. When medicine can help, the doctor explains what it is for, how long it takes to work, possible side effects and how long you may need it, and you decide together.

Are psychiatric medicines addictive?

Most commonly used medicines, including antidepressants, are not addictive in the way people fear. A few, such as some sleeping and anti-anxiety tablets, can cause dependence if taken for long, so doctors use them carefully and usually for a short time. Never stop a prescribed medicine suddenly on your own; ask your doctor how to reduce it safely.

How long does treatment take?

It depends on the problem. Everyday stress or a difficult life event may improve in a few sessions. Depression and anxiety often need weeks to months, and antidepressants usually take two to six weeks to show their full effect. Long-term conditions such as bipolar disorder or schizophrenia need ongoing care, much like diabetes or blood pressure.

How do I know if I have depression or just feel sad?

Sadness passes; depression stays. If most days for two weeks or more you feel low or empty, have lost interest in things you used to enjoy, and also notice changes in sleep, appetite, energy or concentration, or feel hopeless, it is time to see a doctor. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, seek help immediately.

What are the signs of anxiety and panic attacks?

Anxiety can feel like constant worry, overthinking, restlessness, a racing heart, sweating or trouble sleeping. A panic attack brings sudden, intense fear with chest tightness, breathlessness, shaking or dizziness, usually peaking within minutes. Panic attacks are frightening but treatable. If chest pain is new, get it checked medically as well.

When should I see a doctor for sleep problems?

See a doctor if trouble sleeping lasts more than three to four weeks, or if it affects your mood, work, studies or health. Poor sleep is often linked with stress, anxiety, depression or medical causes, and treating the cause works better than only taking sleeping tablets.

Do you treat children and teenagers?

Yes. We assess and support children and teenagers with ADHD, attention and behaviour concerns, autism, school stress, anxiety, low mood and exam pressure. A parent or guardian joins the first visit, and we involve the family throughout.

Can you help with alcohol, tobacco or drug addiction?

Yes. We offer confidential assessment, counselling and medical guidance for alcohol, tobacco and drug use, without judgement. Families are welcome to take part, and we will tell you honestly if a higher level of care, such as a hospital stay, is needed.

My parent is becoming forgetful. Can you help?

Yes. Forgetfulness can be a normal part of ageing, but it can also be a sign of dementia, depression or a treatable medical problem such as a vitamin or thyroid issue. We assess memory and mood, explain the findings to the family in plain language, and guide you on care and support.

Do you treat schizophrenia and bipolar disorder?

Yes. Our severe mental illness care covers schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, delusions, hearing voices and severe suspiciousness. With regular treatment and family support, many people live steady, meaningful lives, so early and continuous care matters.

How can I help a family member who may have a mental health problem?

Listen without judging, and avoid saying "just be strong" or "think positive". Gently suggest seeing a doctor and offer to go with them. Asking directly whether they have thoughts of ending their life does not put the idea in their mind, and it can save a life. You are welcome to call us for guidance.

Do you offer psychotherapy and counselling?

Yes. All of our doctors provide cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) and supportive counselling, in person or online, alongside medical assessment and medicine when appropriate, and Dr. Sunita Lama is also formally certified in CBT. If a specialised therapy would suit you better, we can refer you to a clinical psychologist.

When should someone see a psychiatrist?

See a psychiatrist when emotional, behavioural or thinking symptoms, such as low mood, excessive worry, sleep problems, trouble concentrating, or unusual thoughts or experiences, last more than a few weeks and affect your daily life, work or relationships. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, seek help immediately.

Can mental illness be treated effectively?

Yes. Most mental health conditions, including depression, anxiety disorders, bipolar disorder, OCD and schizophrenia, respond well to evidence-based treatment that combines medicine, psychotherapy and lifestyle support, especially when they are diagnosed early and treated consistently.

Are psychiatric medicines safe?

When prescribed and monitored by a qualified psychiatrist, psychiatric medicines are generally safe and effective. Like all medicines they can have side effects, which is why regular follow-up and open communication with your doctor matter.

Do psychiatric medicines have a lot of side effects?

Like all medicines they can cause side effects, but the risk is often overstated. Most people tolerate modern psychiatric medicines reasonably well, side effects are explained before you start, and the choice, dose and monitoring are tailored to you to keep them as low as possible while still treating the condition.

Will I have to take medicine for life if I start psychiatric medicines?

Not necessarily. It depends on the condition, its severity and how you respond. Many people take medicine for a set period alongside therapy and later reduce it under medical supervision, while people with longer-term or recurring conditions may benefit from continuing for longer. Your doctor reviews this with you regularly rather than deciding once and leaving it unchanged.

Do you assess and treat adult ADHD?

Yes. Our psychiatrists assess ADHD in adults as well as in children and teenagers, using a detailed history and questionnaires, and discuss treatment that may include practical strategies, treatment of related conditions and, for some people, medicine. Read our guide to adult ADHD in Nepal.

Can anxiety cause tingling legs, a racing heart, dry mouth and frequent urination?

Yes. Anxiety triggers the body's alarm system, which can cause tingling or numbness, a pounding heart, dry mouth, frequent urination, sweating and a warm feeling. New or severe symptoms should still be checked by a doctor, because other conditions can look similar. See anxiety symptoms in the body.

Which medicine is used for anxiety?

Doctors commonly use antidepressant-type medicines such as SSRIs, which take a few weeks to work, and sometimes short-term sedatives, which can cause dependence and are used carefully. The right choice depends on you, so never start, stop or share anxiety medicine without a doctor's advice. Read about anxiety treatment and medicine in Nepal.

How is OCD treated?

OCD is usually treated with cognitive behavioural therapy using exposure and response prevention (ERP), often together with medicine such as an SSRI. Treatment brings large improvement for many people. Read our OCD treatment guide.

What is the treatment for insomnia?

Treatment starts with better sleep habits, cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) and treating any cause such as anxiety or depression. Sleeping tablets are used cautiously and only for a short time because they can cause dependence. Read our insomnia guide.

When should I take my child to a psychologist or psychiatrist?

Seek help when a problem lasts, gets worse, or affects school, friendships or family life, for example attention or behaviour difficulties, school refusal, persistent worry or low mood, delayed speech or social difficulties. Talk of self-harm needs urgent attention. Read when to see a child psychologist or psychiatrist.

Do you offer online psychiatric consultation within Nepal?

Yes. All three of our doctors provide online consultation and therapy, by video call (for example Google Meet) or by phone, is available for patients anywhere in Nepal and for Nepalis living abroad. It suits follow-up appointments and many first assessments. Urgent or complex situations may still need an in-person or emergency evaluation.

Can I get a prescription after an online consultation?

Yes, where it is clinically appropriate and in line with standard telepsychiatry practice. Some situations still need an in-person follow-up. A prescription issued in Nepal cannot generally be used abroad, so if you live overseas your doctor will discuss the options with you.

When should I come in person instead of consulting online?

In-person visits are generally advised for severe symptoms, safety concerns, a complex first assessment, or situations that need a physical examination, so that the doctor can evaluate you thoroughly.

Where should I sit for a video consultation?

Choose a private, quiet place where you feel safe and can speak freely, such as your bedroom or a quiet room where you will not be interrupted. If other people are nearby, headphones help. Make sure your phone or computer is charged and your connection is steady, and keep a phone number ready in case the video drops.

Is what I tell the doctor kept private?

Yes. Your conversations and records are confidential. We do not share anything with your family, employer or school without your permission, except in the rare situation where someone's life is at serious risk or the law requires it.

I am worried about what people will say. What should I do?

This is one of the most common worries in Nepal, and you are not alone. Mental health conditions are medical conditions, as real as diabetes or asthma. Your visit is private, you choose who knows, and many people quietly come for help and feel relieved that they did.

Can my family join the session?

Yes, if you want them to. Family can join part or all of a session, in the clinic or online. Some people prefer to come alone first. It is your choice, and we respect it.

Will seeing a psychiatrist affect my job, studies or visa?

Your visit is confidential, and we do not send reports to anyone unless you ask us to. If you need a letter, for example for an employer or a university, talk to your doctor about what is appropriate. Requirements for visas and medical checks differ by country, so check with the relevant authority.

Is the clinic a safe, judgement-free place to talk about difficult things?

Yes. Consultations are confidential and non-judgemental, whether the concern is addiction, relationship difficulty, sexual health, a parenting worry or anything else that feels hard to say aloud. You do not need to have the right words prepared before you book.

What should I bring to my first visit?

Bring any previous prescriptions, medical reports or test results, the names of medicines you take now, and a photo ID if you have one. It also helps to jot down your main worries and when they started. If you cannot bring anything, come anyway.

Can I have follow-up visits online?

Yes. After a first assessment, many follow-ups can be done by video, which is convenient if you live outside Kathmandu or are travelling. Your doctor will tell you when an in-person visit is better.

Can I get a second opinion on my diagnosis or treatment?

Yes. Bring your reports and prescriptions, and a psychiatrist will review them with you, in person or online. A second opinion never means you have to change doctors.

Should I stop or change my current medicine before the first visit?

No. Do not stop or change any medicine on your own before the visit. Bring a list of what you take, or the medicines themselves, so the doctor can review them with you. Any changes are made as part of a proper assessment.
